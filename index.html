About

I am a second-year master's student in the Kotani–Watanabe Laboratory at the University of Yamanashi, Japan. My research focuses on assistive robotics, in particular a powered wheelchair that people with severe physical disabilities can operate using only their gaze.

In my project, TOGAKUSHI2, the user drives a WHILL Model CR powered wheelchair by dwelling their gaze on directional buttons shown on a PC screen. The system combines dwell-based gaze input with obstacle detection using a 3D LiDAR, and it has been trained and evaluated together with an actual user at a special needs school over several months. I care about validating assistive technology in the real environment where it will be used, not only in the laboratory.

My master's thesis is titled 「視線滞留入力式電動車いすの研究開発と特別支援学校での訓練・評価」 (Research and Development of a Dwell-Based Gaze-Controlled Powered Wheelchair and Its Training and Evaluation at a Special Needs School). After graduating in March 2027, I will join Seiko Epson Corporation in April 2027.

Research — TOGAKUSHI2

Dwell-Based Gaze-Controlled Powered Wheelchair with Obstacle Detection

People who cannot use a conventional joystick because of motor disabilities need an alternative way to drive a powered wheelchair. TOGAKUSHI2 uses dwell-based gaze input: looking at an on-screen button for a short dwell time selects it, so the wheelchair can be driven hands-free, without calibration-heavy pointing and without physical switches.

System overview

Gaze input terminal A PC running a graphical interface with large directional buttons. The user selects a button by dwelling their gaze on it; the selected command is sent to the robot side over UDP. (Repository: GUI_TOGAKUSHI2)
Robot control A ROS 2 Humble workspace (19 packages) that receives drive commands, manages the wheelchair state, and generates motion commands. (Repository: ros2_TOGAKUSHI2)
Platform WHILL Model CR powered wheelchair.
Obstacle detection A 3D LiDAR monitors the surroundings of the wheelchair; detected obstacles are fed back to the interface and used to support safe driving.
Path generation Generates a path for passing through doorways without relying on a pre-built map, supporting driving in ordinary school and home environments.
Gaze➜ GUI PC➜ UDP➜ ROS 2➜ WHILL Model CR

Evaluation at a special needs school

The system was evaluated in a real environment: a student at a special needs school used the wheelchair in training and evaluation sessions held over a period of three months (six sessions). The evaluation confirmed that gaze-only driving is feasible in a real school environment and provided longitudinal observations on how the user's driving changes with repeated training.
